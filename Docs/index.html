Lumiform Colorpicker Design Doc & Roadmap

Real-Time 3D Color Picker & Light Simulator for Photoshop 2025

Project Overview & Vision

Lumiform Colorpicker is an interactive 3D color preview and light simulation plugin designed for digital painters, concept artists, and illustrators. It addresses the fundamental flaw of 2D flat color pickers: muddy, unnatural shadows and chalky highlights caused by naive value shifts.

The Problem in 2D Painting

When artists pick shadow colors using traditional 2D swatches, they typically shift brightness down toward black or desaturate. In reality, shadows are illuminated by ambient skylight and ground bounce light.

Failure Mode: Desaturated, dead shadows lacking ambient color temperature and form clarity.

The 3D Solution

By mapping the local base color onto a real-time lit 3D sphere, artists immediately see how warm sunlight, cool sky fill, and ground bounce naturally interact. The artist clicks directly on the sphere or the value ramp to paint with physical light.

Outcome: Vibrant, accurate lighting ramps with instant one-click brush application.

Core Design Pillars

1. In-Panel 3D Viewport

Three.js WebGL viewport rendering a smooth, grounded 3D sphere with wheel zoom, a size slider, and Orbit/Height light-direction sliders.

2. Physical Light Simulation

Directional key light with 3-zone ambient environment (Sky, Horizon, Ground bounce) and instant Time-of-Day presets.

3. Live 5-Value Swatch Ramp

Mathematically accurate real-time swatches: Highlight, Half-Tone, Terminator, Core Shadow, and Bounce Light.

3D & Lighting Physics Engine

3-Zone Ambient & Directional Key Light

Realistic illumination is represented by combining directional sun rays with hemispherical and ground-reflected scatter:

I_total = I_key * max(0, N · L) + I_ambient(N) + I_specular

Where ambient lighting interpolates across three vertical bands:

  • Sky (Zenith): Affects surfaces facing upward ($\hat{N}_y > 0$).
  • Horizon: Affects lateral glancing angles ($\hat{N}_y \approx 0$).
  • Ground Bounce: Affects downward undersides ($\hat{N}_y < 0$).

Surface Finish Modes

The sphere material dynamically switches between three physical finishes:

  • Matte (default): Roughness 1.0, 0% Reflectance. Pure Lambertian diffuse (skin, chalk, matte fabric).
  • Satin: Roughness 0.45, 40% Reflectance. Broad, soft GGX highlight (leather, smooth plastic).
  • Glossy: Roughness 0.08, 95% Reflectance. Tight GGX highlight with Fresnel edge (metal, wet surfaces).

Color Controls & Swatch Ramp

Self-Contained In-Panel Controls

The panel includes dedicated HSB sliders and a Hex input:

  • Hue: 0° to 360° gradient slider.
  • Saturation: 0% to 100% dynamic saturation gradient.
  • Brightness: 0% to 100% value slider.
  • Hex Input: Bidirectional hex sync with validation.

Decoupled Input Guarantee: The 3D sphere is driven strictly by panel controls to prevent unwanted canvas feedback loops while painting.

Analytical 5-Value Swatch Calculation

Rather than reading arbitrary screen pixels, the 5 value swatches are evaluated mathematically from the lighting model at canonical surface normal angles:

Highlight Half-vector $\hat{H} = \text{normalize}(\hat{L} + \hat{V})$
Half-Tone Surface normal at $45^\circ$ toward light vector $\hat{L}$
Terminator Normal perpendicular to light ($\hat{N} \cdot \hat{L} = 0$)
Core Shadow Normal facing opposite light before ground bounce
Bounce Light Normal pointing directly down toward ground reflection

Depth, Atmosphere & Multi-Sphere Composition

The preview is being extended from a single sphere into a three-sphere depth study. The goal is for concept artists to compare how the same lighting behaves at near, mid, and far distances, and to sample atmosphere-shifted colors directly from the render. In Progress

1. Three Depth Slots

Center sphere nearest the camera, right sphere at mid distance, left sphere furthest near the horizon. All spheres start at the same base radius and rest on the ground; perspective creates the initial size difference, and each sphere can then be scaled independently.

2. Aerial Perspective

Distance-based extinction and in-scattering tinted by the horizon sky. Contrast and saturation fall off with distance so the far sphere reads visibly hazier than the mid sphere.

3. Per-Sphere Color

Each sphere has its own albedo. A Link Colors toggle applies one edit to every visible sphere, while unlinked edits affect only the selected sphere.

Composition Slots

Slot Depth On-Screen Role Atmosphere Shadows
Center Near Largest sphere, primary subject, closest to camera Minimal haze, full contrast Strongest contact AO and drop shadow
Right Mid Secondary sphere, partially receding Partial haze, softened contrast Softer, shorter-looking shadow
Left Far Smallest sphere near the horizon line Strong haze, atmospheric color shift Faint shadow, absorbs into ground haze

Controls

Control Behavior
Sphere Count 1 / 2 / 3 selector. Lowering removes the far-left first, then the right; the center always remains. Raising fills the nearest empty slot (right before left) with a fresh copy of the center's current color at 100% size. Existing spheres never move, and deleted state is not restored.
Delete Selecting a non-center sphere enables Delete, which removes exactly that sphere. Delete is disabled while the center is selected. Count buttons update to the resulting number of spheres.
Selected Sphere Click a sphere in the viewport or use the Center / Right / Left selector. The selection persists for editing, while a highlight ring flashes for ~1 second and fades on every selection action. If the selected sphere is deleted, selection falls back to the center.
Per-Sphere Size Selecting a sphere exposes its own Size slider (20%–100%). Scaling pivots on that sphere's bottom-center so it stays grounded; the camera does not move. Link Colors does not link sizes.
Link Colors When enabled, the selected sphere's color is applied to every visible sphere and subsequent edits affect them all. Disabling the link keeps the shared color so spheres can diverge from there.
Color Panel HSB sliders and hex input edit the selected sphere's albedo. The swatch ramp follows the selected sphere.
Eyedropper Samples the final, atmosphere-affected pixel from any sphere, so haze-shifted colors can be sent directly to Photoshop's brush.
Atmosphere Distance-based haze applied consistently to spheres, ground, and shadows; stronger on the far sphere.

Implementation Notes

The ground shader accumulates drop shadows and contact AO across all visible spheres. Each sphere renders with its own material instance so albedo and depth treatment stay independent. Atmosphere is evaluated per fragment from camera distance and blended toward the horizon/sky color; the ground uses the same model so objects and floor share one atmosphere. Picking reads the final framebuffer, which means the sampled color already includes haze. The analytical swatch ramp follows the selected sphere, including its depth haze.
